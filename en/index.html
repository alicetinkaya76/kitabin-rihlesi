What are the digital humanities?

The humanities ask their old questions of material that is now countable, mappable and searchable — that is all. Not a new discipline of its own; it is the muʿjam, fihrist and ṭabaqāt tradition carried on with digital tools. So what do these three words mean in practice?

📊

COUNTABLE

“How often, in what proportion, in which period?” Classical counterpart: muʿjam / concordance A trend can be measured without reading the whole text line by line. Example: the ratio of treaty to force wording in one section of Futūḥ al-buldān — across the book as a whole, entry by treaty stands at 52.2%, yet in this section the balance is reversed. Try the live counter below ↓
🗺

MAPPABLE

“Where, along which route, at what density?” Classical counterpart: the masālik wa-mamālik literature Once a place name is tied to coordinates, the pattern becomes visible. Example: when the 226 conquest records are plotted, entry by treaty clusters in the coastal cities and entry by force in the mountainous regions — the pattern is geographical, not literary. See the map at the GIS stop ↓
🔍

SEARCHABLE

“Where does this phrase occur, and in what context?” Classical counterpart: fihrist / aṭrāf arrangement A single sentence is found among thousands of pages in seconds and shown with its context. Example: the workbench on this site searches for every evidence sentence in the source text and lays the passage before you together with the lines around it. try it yourself at stop 4 ↓

This threefold division is not arbitrary: in Graphs, Maps, Trees (Verso, 2005) Franco Moretti proposed three abstract models for looking at literary history — graphs, maps, trees — in that same order; we apply the same triad to the classical textual tradition. The collection that gathers his conceptual framework is also available in Turkish: Distant Reading (Turkish ed.), trans. Onur Gayretli, İstanbul Bilgi University Press, 2021.

So what is new? What changed is not the question but scale and speed. A. J. Wensinck’s team completed the concordance of ḥadīth wording over half a century; Muḥammad Fuʾād ʿAbd al-Bāqī prepared his muʿjam of the Qurʾān through years of labour. Today a student can produce a comparable index of a work of their own choosing over a weekend. The labour has not decreased; it has moved: from compiling the index to verifying and interpreting what the index yields.

Which questions does it answer? On the left the classical question, on the right the form it takes at digital scale:

Qurʾānic exegesis“In which morphological patterns does this concept occur in the Qurʾān?”→ Root-and-pattern table: every derivative is listed with its verse and context; the divergent choices of two translations are set side by side.
Ḥadīth“At which transmitter do the reports of this chapter converge?”→ Isnād network: transmitters become nodes and chains become edges; the common link (madār) becomes visible through measurement.
Islamic history“Were the cities taken by treaty or by force?”→ Evidence-linked record set: 226 events, each with its city, hijrī year, type and an evidence sentence drawn from the text. The central case study of this site.
Islamic law“How did a single question shift over time across fatwā collections?”→ Time series: the reasoning given for the same question in fatwās from different periods is classified and compared year by year.
Sufism“Into which geography, and in which generation, did a spiritual lineage spread?”→ Network and map together: master–disciple ties are drawn as a network while lodges are given coordinates.
Ottoman & Turkish“When did a concept rise in the periodical press?”→ Frequency chart: the concept’s occurrences in newspapers and journals are plotted by year and the breaks are periodised.

All six are old questions. What changed is the answer’s scale ve verifiability — because every record keeps its evidence tied to the source.

A short history — the roots of this method lie in our own scholarly tradition. Eight stops, in three views: the citation strip, a true-to-scale timeline ve the map. Touch any one of them; all three move together.

The upper axis is true to scale: two stops in a thousand years. The magnifier below opens up the last hundred years: six stops. That is the strip’s single sentence — the method’s lineage is old; its acceleration is new.

In these disciplines the tradition of classification, fihrist and muʿjam has continued for centuries. What has changed is that part of the labour has passed to the machine; method and judgement remain with the researcher.

Let us draw the boundaries at the outset — three common assumptions, three answers:

✗ Assumption: “You can’t get in without knowing how to code.”✓ In truth: This is not a matter of computation but of method . The question, the classification and the judgement are yours; the machine is only a hand. The whole of this site runs without code.
✗ Assumption: “It retires close reading.”✓ In truth: It is the servantof the commentary: the count tells you where to concentrate your attention; the station still belongs to close reading. Two ends of a telescope, one eye.
✗ Assumption: “You ask and it gives the answer.”✓ In truth: It is not an answer machine; what it speeds up is not the answer but the the road to the evidence. Speed without evidence is the speed of error.

📓 Codebook ( codebook) — the rule for each drawer

A codebook is the dataset’s manual: what each field means, which values it may take, how it was derived, whether it may be empty. A dataset without a codebook cannot be read — in six months you will not be able to read it yourself either.

Rows with a red edge are mandatory fields: if one of them is empty, the record does not enter the dataset.

A dataset is not reality itself. EICD-B’s ṣulḥan/ʿanwatan proportions show not what happened in the seventh century but what al-Balādhurī recorded . The article says so by name in its limitations section.
A dataset is not neutral. Which drawers you open is a decision. Whoever opens a “mode of conquest” drawer has declared that the distinction matters; whoever leaves it shut declares something else.
A dataset is never finished. That is exactly what version numbers are for: v1.0 is not a claim but a stopping point. Errors are found, corrected, v1.1 is published — and the old version is not deleted, it remains.

Four concepts done: distant reading · network · GIS · dataset. Now let us look at the order in which a researcher actually uses them ↓

File formats: what are .txt · .md/.mARkdown · JSON · CSV?

.txt ( plain text): a pure text file with no formatting at all; it opens on any device in any program. Digital copies of classical texts are usually in this format.
.md (Markdown): a format that adds a few simple marks to plain text to define headings, lists and emphasis. OpenITI’s .mARkdown is this convention adapted to classical texts: ### | marks a section heading, ~~ a line continuation, and PageV05P022 the volume and page number of the printed edition.
JSON (JavaScript Object Notation): a data format written as “label”: value pairs. This is the machine-side counterpart of the chest of drawers above; both humans and machines read it, it is plain text, and it needs no special software.
CSV (Comma-Separated Values): stores table rows as plain text; it opens directly in Excel and Google Sheets. A dataset is often published as JSON and CSV at the same time.

The life story of a record:PASSAGE→INSTRUCTION→RECORD (you are here)→VERIFICATION→FIGURE

This record has 225 siblings in the chest; 91.6% of the evidence was located in its source (EICD-B). Once the chest is full, all three lenses work at once: counting, mapping and network all feed from here.

For the curious: why these five drawers? — a defence of the schema

Why is the year a number? So that it can be sorted and plotted; a drawer that says “early period” produces no chart. Why is the type a closed list? Free text cannot be counted; three gates discipline the debate — a borderline case goes to “undecided” rather than being ignored. Why is evidence mandatory? Unless it is mandatory, the chest becomes an elegant-looking heap of conjecture. Why does provenance get its own drawer? If the chain book → edition → digital version → dataset breaks, in five years nobody can reproduce the figures. The schema is the first draft of your thesis: which drawers you open reveals what you consider a question.

To predict well at this scale it had to compress grammar, registers and factual patterns. On top of this base floor it put on two more — open the three drawers in order:

It learns from trillions of words with a single objective: predict the next word. “Just statistics” — true; but at this scale statistics really are powerful. Belittling misleads as much as exaggerating.
Humans score the answers and the model is tuned towards what is liked. This is the source both of its politeness and of its confident tone — sounding certain scores points.
Search, document upload, tool and database connections. What distinguishes one product from another is usually this layer — the “Your turn” workbench at the fourth stop also works by putting the source in front of the model .

What is MCP? ( Model Context Protocol) — an open protocol that lets a chat model connect to external sources in a standard and auditable way; think of it as a plug-in for the chat interface. Once a connection is in place the model can run a direct query against sources such as a national thesis centre, a journal platform or a library catalogue; it answers from the records that source returns rather than from memory, and you can see which query ran. It does not lift the burden of evidence — you still verify the citation at its source — but it does remove the copy-and-paste labour in between.
Why does it fabricate? Like a student in an exam with no penalty for wrong answers: guessing pays better than silence. Fabrication (hallucination) is not a fault but a structural risk — its antidote is the collation you are about to see.
Why does it always agree with you? It was tuned to be liked. Pleasant speech is no mark of true speech — be wary of an adviser who never objects.

Now let us turn these clues into engineering: five steps, all of them interactive. Our sample sentence is the departure of the traveller you met at the map stop: Ibn Baṭṭūṭa’s Tangier record.

1

tokenizationDoes it read Arabic? No: first it splits, then it converts to numbers

Input: letters → Output: token numbers
A sentence from the book
The same sense, in English

The model sees neither letters nor words; it sees token numbers . Arabic is split into more tokens than English — the engineering root of linguistic inequality. the split is illustrative — try the real thing with your own sentence at tiktokenizer. And note: ة and ه carry different numbers — the PARTIAL matches you will meet at the “Your turn” stop trace back to exactly this.

For the curious: how was this split chosen?

The method is called sub-word tokenization(the BPE family): the vocabulary builds itself from the letter sequences that occur most often in the training texts and typically runs to 100,000–250,000 tokens. Because Arabic is richly inflected, the same meaning is split into more tokens — which affects both cost and how quickly the context window fills. A given token always has the same number; “meaning” does not live in that number but begins on the next floor.

2

embeddingIt places every token on a map of meaning

Input: token numbers → Output: points in a space of meaning

Not a dictionary but a the map: during training every token acquires a place in this space; related senses land close together. فتح neighbours both districts — “to open” or “to conquer”? The next step decides. the layout is illustrative — in reality there are thousands of dimensions; we are seeing two.

For the curious: what exactly is a “point”?

Every token is a vector: a list of thousands of numbers (an embedding). “Closeness” is the similarity of these lists, measured by cosine. Below, in Down to the core · Floor 1, you can set the number strips of two words side by side and have the similarity computed yourself — riḥla and safar (journey) comes out at 0.99.

3

attentionAs it writes each word it weighs the whole passage

Input: points → Output: meaning weighted by context

The words that carry weight light up — فتحthis weighing decides which district of siyāq–sibāq (reading by context) wins. The Qurʾān commentator calls this reading by context; here the same work, mechanised. the emphases are illustrative — for a live version see Transformer Explainer.

For the curious: who wrote these weights?

Nobody. The mechanism is called self-attention(self-attention): every token asks every other token in the passage “how much should I look at you?”; the weights are not hand-written rules but values that emerged during training. And there is not one scale: on every floor dozens of “heads” look at the same sentence with different eyes — Down to the core · Floor 2, try the “who?” head beside the “when?” head.

The whole art lies in setting up a beginning in which the most likely continuation is the correct record — rather like asking a poet given a metre to complete the line. For live probability bars see: Transformer Explainer.

For the curious: the mathematics of the race

Generation is autoregressive: one single token is chosen at a time, appended to the sentence, and the whole computation runs again. The scores of the race (logits) are turned into probabilities by softmax; “temperature” spreads that distribution out or sharpens it. Down to the core · Floor 3, pull the slider and play with a REAL softmax: at T=0.2 the favourite takes 99.6%; at T=2, 43%.

5

constrained decodingNarrow the gates, leave copying free

Input: a free race → Output: a schema-bound decision carrying evidence
✓ VERBATIM FROM THE TEXT
The copied sentence is now testable: search for it in the book with Ctrl+F — either it is found, or the record falls. In a moment, at the fourth stop, YOU will do exactly this ↓

Reciting the evidence from memory is forbidden; showing it from what lies in front of it is free — copying is easier than inventing; the model takes the easy path, and we tie the easy path to the true one.

For the curious: who closes the gates?

The technique is called constrained decoding / schema-guided output: only permitted tokens are allowed into the race — a three-valued vocabulary, valid JSON and a mandatory “evidence” field all belong to this family. The evidence requirement additionally harnesses the model’s strongest instinct — copying from context — in the service of accuracy; EICD-B’s 91.6% source verification (35.0% of records found verbatim plus 56.6% with orthographic variation) is the work of these two screws.

Nowhere in these five steps is there “a mind that knows Arabic” — there are tokens, numbers, a map, a weighing, a race. Yet their sum produces behaviour that uses Arabic fluently. Is that “understanding”? A question for philosophy. What we measure is: constrained and verified behaviour.
